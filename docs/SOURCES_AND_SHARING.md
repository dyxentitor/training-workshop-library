# Sources, Attribution, and Sharing

[Return to the library](../README.md)

## Research records

Use `research/references.md` to record the source title, publisher, URL, publication or update date, checked date, and the claim it supports. If the publisher does not give a date, record `Not stated`.

Prefer official documentation, original incident reports, and original research for technical claims. If a claim relies on a secondary report, identify that report and its underlying source where available. Label a proposed explanation as an interpretation when the source does not establish it.

Keep short citations on the relevant slide and complete links in the reference log. Check time-sensitive claims before the next delivery.

## Scenarios and demonstrations

Label invented characters, emails, companies, and events as fictional training examples. Identify documented incidents with their sources. Use sample accounts and synthetic records in demonstrations. Run technical exercises in the agreed training environment.

## Images and external material

Record where an image or external resource came from and the permission or licence that permits your use. Prefer links to third-party reports rather than uploading entire reports. Retain required attribution when adapting material.

## Keep delivery records separate

Store participant names, attendance, assessment results, internal incident reports, SMTP credentials, and client-specific screenshots outside the repository. Use generic reporting examples in reusable materials, and adapt them for a specific delivery through a separate approved copy.

## Private master and public collection

A private master collection can hold your reusable working materials. If you want a public portfolio, create a separate repository containing only reviewed, shareable materials. Review the complete history before changing an existing private repository to public: deleting a file from the latest version can leave it in earlier commits.

Repository access applies to the collection, not a selected workshop folder. For participants, share the relevant handout or workshop package through an appropriate channel rather than granting access to the master repository. Personal private repositories give invited collaborators write access; use collaborator invitations for trusted coauthors.

## Reuse terms

No general reuse licence has been selected for this starter. Before publishing reusable content, decide the permissions you intend to grant and ensure you own or can license the relevant material. Document any exceptions for third-party assets. Add the selected licence and update the root README when you make that decision.
