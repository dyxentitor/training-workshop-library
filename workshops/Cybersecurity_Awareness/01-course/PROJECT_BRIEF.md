# Project brief

Create a complete interactive HTML training deck on impersonation and phishing for a full-day 10am–5pm session. Intended learners are ordinary employees and managers, with role-relevant decisions for finance, HR, operations and IT. First delivery is to government-sector participants; keep the core sector-neutral for reuse.

## Required outcomes
Participants can distinguish cloning from account compromise, identify the requested sensitive action, independently verify identity/purpose, check what an approval grants, maintain authorisation procedures, report uncertainty or a mistake, and follow role-appropriate recovery.

## Teaching approach
Use recurring fictional colleagues at Meranti, short scenes, realistic mock evidence, trainer-controlled decisions, revealable findings and documented case comparisons. Include genuine requests that still need verification. Reward sound processes rather than calling everything phishing. Use supportive reporting language without public blame or rankings.

## Scope
Seven modules plus a six-slide opening, 58 learning screens and 3 intermissions. Include social-media identity, public context, brand/manager/vendor/helpdesk impersonation, voice/video, email/mobile/QR/files, MFA/session/device-code/consent/linking, fake support/repair/callback, tabletop, reporting and recovery. Technical details live in notes. Physical security, USB threats and Wi-Fi are outside the core unless explicitly requested later.

## Delivery
Self-contained offline HTML final deck, complete content and notes, source index, printable participant reference and facilitator guide. No external application accounts or live attacks are needed. Use current prototype as source code and visual foundation, not just inspiration.

## Known unknowns
Customer name/brand assets, presentation date and venue, prayer timetable, size/departments, actual email/chat UI, approved reporting and support procedures. English is the draft language matching the prototype. Do not silently translate. Build with placeholders and keep these inputs separate from the core.
