# 35-minute tabletop: Stop the chain

## Preparation
Assign operations, finance, manager and IT roles at each table. Small groups can combine roles. No learner laptop is required. Provide the following cards one at a time. The exercise is a fictional composite with possible connections requiring investigation, not proof of a particular attack chain.

## Evidence cards
T1 (6 minutes): Aina had a normal conversation with a project contact and receives a document invitation. Asked: what is known, what remains unverified, and what action is requested? Do not reveal T2 yet.

T2 (7 minutes): Aina reports entering a supplied code on a sign-in page. Asked: who should know now, what facts are needed, and what access might require review? Preferred: immediate internal report, note time/device/action, IT assesses relevant sessions/grants. Do not ask for her actual code.

T3 (7 minutes): Finance receives a supplier bank-change request plus an apparent manager chat. Asked: what should finance hold, whom to contact and through which records? Preferred: hold change/payment, established vendor callback, normal approvals, preserve evidence.

T4 (7 minutes): A person using Mei's name offers remote help without a known ticket. Asked: what support authorisation is missing, and how does the group report the combined observations? Preferred: verify independently; avoid unverified control; report facts and uncertainty.

Setup 3 minutes; cards 27 minutes; debrief 5 minutes = 35 minutes.

## Required group output
Known facts; unverified claims; next safe actions with owners; a concise incident report. Groups must not conclude one event caused another without evidence. Record hypothesis separately.

## Rubric (0–2 each)
1. Identifies the sensitive action and access/payment consequence.
2. Chooses independent established verification.
3. Maintains approval and data-sharing requirements.
4. Reports promptly with useful facts and an owner.
5. Avoids further unsafe interaction and preserves evidence.

0: absent/unsafe. 1: partly correct with missing route or ownership. 2: specific and appropriate. Ten-point course rubric, not a NIST certification score.

## Debrief
Ask which action interrupted risk and which uncertainty IT still needs to investigate. Thank the reporter. Compare a fast useful report with waiting for a complete diagnosis. Do not display individual rankings or shame a table for an incorrect choice.
