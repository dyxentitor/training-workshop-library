# Facilitator guide

## Before the day
Confirm audience size, language, screen/projector, date/venue and prayer arrangements. Obtain the customer's reporting/support workflow and a safe screenshot if available. Rehearse locally, fullscreen, navigation and long reveal states. Keep the standalone HTML and printed response cards available. Use discussion/show of hands when learners have no devices. Confirm timing rather than improvising extra modules.

## Opening
Say: “Today we will practise handling requests involving information, account access and money. Some messages are genuine. We will focus on how to verify and what to do if we already acted.” Explain fictional Meranti and the separate documented cases. No learner needs to disclose a personal incident.

## Delivery rhythm
Show evidence without the answer. Ask for decisions and reasons. Reveal context only after discussion. Ask how the safer route works in the learner's actual job. Avoid praising people only for identifying phishing. Explain why convenient options feel plausible. Allow 'not enough evidence; verify' as a sound response.

## Source statements
Keep dates and attributed facts visible for documented cases. Do not say all attacks use AI, all MFA fails, or most breaches begin with spear phishing. Discuss the genuine sign-in/device-code trap in terms of authorising someone else's session. Refer technical questions to notes without consuming the entire exercise.

## Role practice
Government-sector learners can use their own approved examples in discussion. The core uses ordinary workplace roles. Finance checks payment change and approval; HR checks data sharing; managers support procedural pause; IT verifies identity/recovery/tickets. Do not invent sector-specific law or obligations.

## Reporting demonstration
At SL56, use the confirmed route. If not supplied, present the labelled generic demonstration and tell the customer owner what needs to be inserted before actual delivery. Show finding the report action, preserving original evidence, writing time/action/device and contacting another channel if access is affected. No report is submitted during the demo.

## After a mistake
Say: “Thank you for reporting. Let’s record what happened so the right team can respond.” Do not ask for passwords, tokens or codes. Distinguish clicked-only, credential/approval, executed software/remote control and payment. Follow the customer's response procedure. Leave containment/investigation to the responsible team.

## Time control
Use the per-slide timings and protect activities. Optional detail belongs in notes. If prayer requires a longer interval, update the schedule rather than rushing the reporting segment. Avoid automatic countdown or slide advancement. Long questions may use follow-up.

## Notes visibility
The prototype's notes appear on the same screen; do not open them expecting privacy while projecting. Use this guide or printed module scripts for private reference.

## Evaluation
Use baseline reasoning in M0 and parallel post-session scenarios. Use the ten-point tabletop rubric. No public individual scores or identifiable quiz collection is required. A 30-day refresher is a proposed programme step, not an automated task or authorised live phishing campaign.
