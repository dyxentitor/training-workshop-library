# Assessment and follow-up

M0 is the baseline: participants choose an action for the payment request and explain why their verification route is trustworthy. This evaluates reasoning, not prior jargon knowledge.

M7 includes a parallel, different-surface situation: verified HR contact requests sensitive information. Ask what authorisation and sharing route is required. Check that learners can transfer the verification principle beyond invoices.

Additional post-session questions, if time allows:
1. Unexpected MFA request: deny and report, without disabling MFA.
2. A genuine service page asks for a code from an unsolicited message: check whose session is being authorised.
3. A colleague reports approving something: thank them and record time/action/device, then escalate through the confirmed route.
4. Supplier confirms new details through established contact: complete required authorisation; verification alone does not permit bypass.

Score whether the learner identifies the action, uses an independent route, follows authorisation and reports/recover appropriately. A proposed 80% target can be set by the programme owner but is not a mandatory standard or proof of real-world prevention. The package does not impose a pass mark.

Follow up after about 30 days with a fresh scenario and reporting-route recall. If an authorised simulation is later used, consider difficulty/recipient context via the NIST Phish Scale, reporting rate and time to useful report. Open tracking/clicks can be affected by scanners and client behaviour. Do not equate clicks with credential exposure or use one campaign as proof of individual negligence.

No scheduling, sending or identifiable assessment collection is authorised by building this deck. Those are separate operational activities.
